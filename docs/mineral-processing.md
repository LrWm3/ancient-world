# Mineral-specific iron processing

After enabling shared extraction sources, choose **Enable mineral-specific iron processing**, or call `Generator::enable_mineral_processing()`. This is an explicit prospective conversion: remaining source mass is identified from the terrain's mineral ID; previously mined generic ore, metal, tools and cargo retain their identities and quantities. The transition records a historical event. Old archives still default to generic ore.

The first chains cover hematite, magnetite and limonite. Goods slots 32–34 become distinct ores, and each gets a fuel-consuming metalworking recipe. Those slots must be unused, including existing inventories, cargo and recipes. Generated recipes use the geological catalog's yield fraction with an 80% recovery factor, half a kilogram of charcoal per kilogram of ore, and respectively 0.12, 0.14 and 0.18 worker-months per kilogram. These are initial game coefficients, not calibrated industrial process measurements. Recipes are archived with the economic catalog and can be edited subject to mass, C/N/P, modeled iron-content and labor validation.

The source registry retains one finite source per terrain cell. Mining consumes that source's monthly allowance and creates the corresponding raw good. Work-order planning considers locally extractable inputs when choosing between recipes, comparing cost per unit output; this forecast does not add inventory or eliminate extraction labor. Adaptive GPU staffing, storage constraints, supplier quotes, shipments and ordinary goods ledgers use the same raw-good index. Smelted output enters the existing general metal stock, supporting tools, equipment and other downstream crafts. Processing losses remain in the existing cumulative material-loss counter.

Coal, phosphates, copper minerals and other unsupported minerals retain their source identity and finite unmined mass, but cannot supply the generic iron-processing chain. A location without a supported iron source must use existing stocks or obtain material through trade. The strongest mapped mineral is the only identified source here; minor coexisting deposits are not represented. Later settlements cannot refresh or relabel an existing identified source.

The planet inspector and exported regional source snapshots expose the same mineral identity and remaining mass. Active raw-good IDs cannot be relabeled by replacing the economic catalog.

Tests cover three iron minerals plus coal and apatite, finite raw withdrawals, expected processing yields, rejection of iron creation from timber, demand-driven tool production, supplier availability, material budgets and exact save/batch continuation. These are controlled fixtures; natural multi-seed economic calibration under restricted iron access is still needed.

The optional [alloy and residue extension](alloy-processing.md) adds copper/tin/bronze, distinct copper/bronze tools and scrap, and bounded persistent residue deposits. Remaining extensions include multiple deposits per source cell, wider equipment substitution, chemically resolved waste and batch provenance through finished objects. This increment does not turn generic historical metal into retrospectively identified iron.

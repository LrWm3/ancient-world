# Local offices with fiscal consequences

`Generator::enable_offices()`, the desktop's **Establish local offices** button,
and `--offices` establish a present-day baseline of local stewardships. Enable
society, politics and governance first. Old histories retain their previous behavior
until explicitly enabled; no earlier appointments are fabricated.

Each settlement has one persistent office identity, jurisdiction and succession
record. A four-year tenure ends on expiry, death, departure, abandonment, or a change
of controller or selection rule. The office itself survives. Vacancies are filled
quarterly from existing living adult local representatives. No new population,
treasury or personal wealth is generated.

Below 75% autonomy, the ruler appoints an eligible local representative from their
civilization, favoring loyalty and caution. Autonomous councils favor generosity
and loyalty among local representatives of any origin. Knowledge, ambition and
recent paid campaigns also influence selection. These are explicit game rules,
not historically fitted selection probabilities. Existing campaign support and
payment requirements remain in force. Appointment events link the paid campaign
when it contributed; tenures preserve person and office references.

A staffed office collects 75–100% of the existing tax assessment, depending on the
holder's knowledge breadth. A vacant office retains 50% collection through unnamed
staff. The existing autonomy discount still applies. Uncollected money remains in
private stocks. The same capacity affects funded administrative loyalty recovery;
vacancies add a small persistent administrative penalty. Existing administrative
payroll is retained. This is a coordination effect, not extra production or money.

Conquest changes jurisdiction and eligibility without converting local religion
or cultural identity. A conquered post may remain vacant until eligible residents
arrive or autonomy permits local selection. The explorer shows holder, rule,
capacity and recent tenures. Archives preserve all terms and vacancy causes.

Controlled tests cover opposed ruler/council choices, actual paid campaigning,
conquest, autonomy changes, exact tax transfers and legacy loading. A five-year run
crosses term expiration and compares uninterrupted, monthly and checkpoint-resumed
histories exactly. The model remains one local civil office, not a full hierarchy
of judges, military ranks or institution officers. Multi-site institutional branches
and constitutional disputes remain separate work.

## Matched century checks

Seeds 17 and 409 ran with and without offices using the same verified executable,
living environment, 16 starting civilizations, resolution 64 and yield 0.5.

| Seed | Final population, control → offices | Shortage town-years, control → offices | Vacant annual observations |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 17 | 2,226 → 2,212 | 865 → 769 | 43 / 2,053 |
| 409 | 2,791 → 2,891 | 618 → 592 | 39 / 1,877 |

Post-baseline events included 548/493 appointments and 539/485 vacancies. Initial
appointments precede the evaluator's event baseline; these counts are subsequent
turnovers, not a census of posts. Maximum relative ledger residual was 1.84e-5.

These populations are downstream nonlinear outcomes. The controlled tax fixture
establishes the immediate fiscal transfer; the seed ensemble does not establish
that offices universally reduce shortages or improve population. Selection rules
and capacity coefficients remain game balancing, not empirical historical fitting.
Most sampled posts remain staffed, while conquest and autonomy cause traceable
vacancies and jurisdiction changes. No minimum frequency of civil failure was imposed.

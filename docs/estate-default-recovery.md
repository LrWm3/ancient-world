# Automatic recovery from closed estate cash

Closed operators and inactive institutions now allocate opening cash across both
live borrowing debt and remaining recorded default losses. Both claim types have
equal rank in this limited policy: each receives its proportion of the same opening
cash, capped at its remaining amount. Incoming payments cannot enlarge another
estate's allocation merely because that estate is processed later in the pass.

Live debt uses the existing early-repayment operation. Defaulted debt uses the
separate recovery operation: original default entries, contracted parties, losses
and default exclusion remain unchanged, and no interest resumes. Actual receipts
name the dated creditor and record both account balances. Claims are not income
or spendable cash; only the transferred recovery reaches a beneficiary's account.

There is no operating reserve for a closed estate. Relocating institutions remain
excluded because their identity and business continue. Active businesses continue
to use the separate opt-in late-export policy, with its operating reserve and
proceeds cap. Estate recovery does not grant new borrowing eligibility.

Unpaid or untransferable balances remain in the estate. Residual cash and creditor
claims cannot reach owners until both live debt and recoverable default losses
are cleared. A precision-blocked zero transfer is recorded, but the same unchanged
attempt need not append another receipt at every same-month estate window.
Changed balances, allowance or a later month permit retry. Repeated passes can use
newly arrived cash; they do not replay the original transfer.

This is a proportional cash-distribution policy, not a general bankruptcy model.
It does not sell remaining physical property, resolve contested beneficiaries,
cancel a debtor's inherited claim against itself, or impose household liability.
Long-run balance effects remain unestablished: the previous ensembles contained
no relevant estate defaults, and are not evidence for this new recovery policy.

## Verification

The mixed-claim hardware fixture gives a closed operator ten cash units against
two twenty-unit claims, one live and one defaulted. Each receives five. The
original default record stays unchanged; the live balance falls to fifteen.
Serialized continuation matches, repeating the boundary cannot replay payments,
and later incoming cash clears retained losses before beneficiary assignment.

The corrected money-residual checks pass in 19 CPU market tests, two hardware
credit-account/production tests, one hardware persisted-credit test, and four
hardware estate-related tests. Thirteen export-contract tests and strict
all-target Clippy also pass. These are focused accounting and integration checks,
not an ensemble calibration of estate recoveries. See the
[residual assertion audit](monetary-residual-audit.md).

# Warnings carried by travelers

A relocating household held by a route closure records the observed month and
route endpoints. This evidence travels with the existing funded journey. It
does not enter local memory until survivors arrive or return; an extinct party
cannot deliver it. The arrival event links the observation to its witnesses.

The receiving settlement's preference for later travel over that connection is
multiplied by a bounded penalty, initially up to 60%, halving its influence after
six months of evidence age. Other settlements do not acquire the warning
automatically. Duplicate delivery cannot refresh it. Existing food testimony
still contributes separately, and physical closure/capacity checks still decide
whether departure is possible. A warning discourages but does not prohibit escape.

This first warning concerns an encountered closure, not a global danger map,
hostile-occupation intelligence or a disease diagnosis. Both in-transit evidence
and received memory are archived. Missing fields in older journeys mean unknown
experience, not an invented safe journey.

Checks include locality, delayed receipt, aging, replay and serialization, plus
the existing relocation fixture with a temporary closure and surviving arrival.

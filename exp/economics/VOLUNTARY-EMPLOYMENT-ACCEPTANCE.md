# Voluntary employment in the financed economy

CPU and reference runs pass the unchanged 48-month readiness gates.

This target replaces the two preaccepted employment arrangements in the
[nine-agent financed economy](WORKING-CAPITAL-ACCEPTANCE.md) with dated employer
requests and worker acceptance. The workshop and town must find useful, affordable
work, and the person must find supplying the hours no worse than retaining them. They use
ordinary employment delivery, wage claims, settlement and double-entry accounting.

This is a bounded recurring hiring experiment. Parties, capacities, prices, legal
permissions, possible employment terms and constitutional menus remain supplied.
The new decision is whether, and how much, to accept **this month**. It is not
anonymous recruiting, wage negotiation or discovery of arbitrary counterparties.
Other scenarios retain their existing behavior unless explicitly configured.

## Admission and monthly boundaries

An organization's signed operating configuration can contain a hiring mandate:
a set of eligible offer IDs, a payroll ceiling in one resource and a horizon.
A worker separately opts into evaluating offers with a horizon and ordered
stock-reserve objectives. Existing legal capacity-trade permission remains necessary. Configuring
a mandate does not create an earned wage, reserve labor or sign all future jobs.

- Open retains the existing organization work/policy selection and capacity reset.
- Acquire evaluates optional employment after actual acquisitions and earlier
  reservations. The preview includes those results, including household pooling.
  It does not re-spend the opening balance. A previously dated production plan
  blocks this additional hiring path rather than silently changing its grants.
- Feasible quantities are considered largest first, bounded by the worker's
  remaining capacity, the offer, current funds, existing wage claims and remaining
  payroll budget. Prior payments in another denomination do not spend this budget.
- The employer must project strictly better governing outcomes, more current-month
  completed one-period productive work and enough additional capacity use to justify
  the hours. An unusable partial task and idle paid hours fail this check.
- The worker must project no worse lexicographic outcomes and no worse individual
  need, process-failure or protected-obligation measures. The reserve tie-breaks
  may trade off against each other when earned cash buys food. Cash alone cannot compensate
  for an additional essential-need deficit.
- Accepted capacity is delivered through the existing employment exchange. The
  ordinary claim is earned then and paid at Close. The forecast requires full
  payment at that original Close, not eventual recovery after an arrear.

Both parties protect the listed deliveries, loans and wage duties. This is not a
universal guarantee for every agreement type: land dues and guarantees are not
additional components of these hiring duty vectors. Actual settlement still handles
those arrangements through their existing adapters.

The explicit allocation policy remains rank, then agreement ID. Later candidates
see prior reservations and cannot reuse the same hours or money. This is sequential
admission, not an optimized portfolio of all employers' competing bids. Mandates
support at most eight offers, eight hours per offer and twelve forecast months.

## Forecasts and live consent

`DatedJob` valuation compares accepting the current job against declining it,
retaining actual commitments and disabling later unsigned hiring in both branches.
It uses the maximum of the supplied employer/worker horizons. The employer and
worker score their own interests under the same recorded public peer-expectation
snapshot from the employer's operating configuration. They do not yet choose
independent belief models. Observed peer work and election hypotheses apply only
at subsequent Open boundaries; receipts distinguish attempts from completion.

Broader operating, financing and startup-forward forecasts may need to test whether
an economy could function with labor. Their frozen worlds permit
`ConditionalFeasibility`: a nonrecursive one-month test of physical completion,
useful hours and funded payroll. It does not predict utility-based consent.
Conditional labor is recorded explicitly in projections, never as a live signature.
The real Acquire must still obtain both parties' acceptance. A forecast can hire a
worker who subsequently refuses; only the live outcome creates real claims.

This separation addresses a measured failure. Allowing the current-job comparison
to substitute hypothetical hiring next month repeatedly postponed useful work
now. Production stalled despite positive productive alternatives. We did not fix
this by forcing acceptance on ties or overriding governing objectives. A separate
trial disabled future hiring but also ignored observed customer work; it sustained
an initially intermittent economy. Reusing the configured public customer observations makes those assumptions
explicit in hiring as well; on its own, it did not improve the observed throughput.
The actual remaining refusals came from cash-only worker preferences: earning a
wage triggered a three-grain purchase, reducing closing cash while increasing food
stocks. The positive fixture therefore supplies a three-grain reserve goal before
the cash goal. This is an explicit food-security preference, not universal wealth
valuation. A matched cash-only control retains the refusal.

These assumptions remain limited. The dated comparison may reject a valuable job
whose benefits require another unsigned future job. Conditional labor in broader
forecasts can overestimate supply. Neither is a universal planning solution. Pantry-first ordering can prefer food
to cash near the horizon cutoff; the reserve list is supplied, bounded to eight
unique stock resources, and not inferred as a universal preference model.

## Startup resources

The old fixture paid both standing employment arrangements even during idle startup.
Removing those payments exposes a real worker food gap. The new positive fixture
allocates three additional initial grain units to the worker **from the state's
existing grain**, keeping total grain and all coin balances unchanged. There is no
recurring food grant, additional mint, waived debt or later forced transfer.
The original distribution remains available as a failing startup control.

This is an explicit scenario calibration, not endogenous startup welfare or credit.
The worker still earns wages, purchases food and meets ordinary monthly needs after
that initial stock is used.

## Measured comparisons

The positive 48-month CPU and reference runs each complete **44 mints, 45 refining jobs and
46 mining jobs**, issuing 440 coin units. It has zero food deficits, no historical
wage or loan arrears, both household forward units delivered on time, annual rent
paid at the required dates, and all nine books reconciled. The workshop pays 45
wage coins and the town pays 46; there are no standing idle-startup wages.

| Operating interval | Refining / mining jobs | Metal sales | Replacement ore | Workshop / town income | Workshop / town closing cash |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Months 13–24 | 12 / 12 | 12 | 24 | 12 / 13 | 25 / 31 |
| Months 25–36 | 12 / 12 | 12 | 24 | 12 / 13 | 37 / 44 |
| Months 37–48 | 12 / 12 | 12 | 24 | 12 / 13 | 49 / 57 |

Both employers retain enough cash for their next operating package. Each accepted
job has live employer and worker approval, useful current productive hours and a
fully paid wage. None of the original readiness thresholds were relaxed.

The initial 48-month conditional-future-hiring trial completed only one mint and
recorded 79 aggregate food-deficit units despite the startup food allocation.
The dated-job, cash-only trial completed 38 mints, 39 refining jobs and 40 mining
jobs with no food deficits or arrears. It failed the original first-operating-year
throughput gates: only nine jobs per employer in months 13–24. The subsequent two
years each had twelve. Keeping the employer's observed peer model did not change
those totals. These trials motivated the continuation and stock-preference changes;
they are not counted as passing acceptance results.

In the matched first twelve months, food-then-cash preferences produce nine
refining jobs, ten mining jobs and eight mints, versus six, seven and five with
cash-only preferences. Both runs use the same resources, terms, allocation policy,
food distribution and horizons.

The temporary-absence control removes the worker's capacity in month 15. It creates
no earned wage that month, and both employers resume hiring afterward. Through
month 24 it records 19 mints, 20 refining jobs and 21 mining jobs, zero observed food
deficits and wage arrears, and nine reconciled books. The test asserts no phantom
wage, resumed work, no wage arrears and reconciliation; it does not certify the
uninterrupted 48-month throughput gates for this shocked run.

Removing worker participation or both payroll budgets prevents mining, refining
and minting in the twelve-month controls. No employment is earned, and nutrition
fails. Removing the conserved startup food allocation also leaves unmet nutrition.
The focused tests separately retain essential-need refusal, missing work/funds/legal
permission, shared finite hours, unusable partial work, denomination isolation,
receipt integrity and visible conditional forecasts that do not override refusal.

## Verification

The acceptance test retains the original 48-month readiness gates: nutrition,
three complete operating years, actual recurring labor and outputs, replacement
inputs, positive income, finite operating reserves, timely obligations and nine
separate reconciled books. Passing local consent tests alone is insufficient.

Commands from `exp/economics`:

```sh
cargo +1.92.0 test --locked --release -j8 --test voluntary_hiring
cargo +1.92.0 test --locked --release -j8 --test voluntary_employment -- --nocapture
cargo +1.92.0 run --locked --release -j8 --example voluntary_employment -- 48 --cpu
cargo +1.92.0 run --locked --release -j8 --example voluntary_employment -- 12 --original-allocation
```

`--details` prints the decision receipts. The planning observer also exposes the
valuation, authority, physical ceiling, affordable quantity, actual employer demand,
mutual acceptance, objective/duty identities, public expectation evidence and
alternative refusal reasons. These records are attached to the existing boundary;
no tracing statements are scattered through economic process implementations.

All four integration tests passed. The 48-month CPU run restores the simulation
after every phase and exactly matches reference world, state, ledger, monthly
reports and financial audit. Startup ledger replay matches every phase through
month 12; reversing independent actor, resource, definition, employment and market
catalogs matches the same twelve-month state, ledger and audit. Monthly stepping
and batched continuation also agree over that startup window. This exercises
in-memory reconstruction, not a new serialized checkpoint format.

The focused and regression command passed 137 tests, including the previous
48-month financed-startup acceptance. Strict all-target Clippy and the repository
artifact check passed: **141 targeted tests in total**. The integration bundle took
about 22 minutes on this host; this is an observed test duration, not a performance
benchmark. Independent review checked forecast isolation, duties,
monthly boundaries, reserve preferences and the control interpretation.

These are bounded correctness checks, not a scaling benchmark. The employment
search and private economic forecasts run in Rust; selecting CubeCL CPU exercises
state-effect execution. Conditional feasibility limits recursive reasoning, but
repeated forecasts and validation still make the integrated comparison costly.
This result does not establish large-population or GPU planning performance.
